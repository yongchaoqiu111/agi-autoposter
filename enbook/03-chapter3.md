# Chapter 3: What Industrial-Grade Systems Really Need

## 3.1 Certainty > Intelligence

Intelligence is a means; certainty is the end.

**A stupid but certain result is better than a smart but uncertain result.**

## 3.2 Controllability > Evolution Capability

Controllability means: you know what the system is doing, you know why it does it, you know what it will do next.

Evolution capability means: you do not know.

**Industrial-grade systems choose controllability.**

## 3.3 Observability > Self-Adaptation

Observability means: all operations have logs, all states are queryable, all errors are traceable.

Self-adaptation means: the system decides how to do things, and you may not know.

**Industrial-grade systems choose observability.**

## 3.4 Clear Responsibility > Automating Everything

Not everything should be automated. Some decisions require human sign-off, some operations require human confirmation, some boundaries must not be crossed.

**Industrial-grade systems allow "automation," but only on the premise of "clear responsibility."**

---

## Chapter Summary

| Choose | Abandon |
|--------|---------|
| Certainty | Intelligence |
| Controllability | Evolution capability |
| Observability | Self-adaptation |
| Clear responsibility | Automating everything |

Core requirements of industrial-grade systems: stability, controllability, observability, and clear responsibility.
